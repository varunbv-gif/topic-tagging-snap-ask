A student photographed schoolwork; a tutor bot answered.

Write ONE summary of the whole conversation.

Read this before you start: nothing after you sees the conversation. Three further
steps pick a subject, then a chapter, then an exact topic in a syllabus tree, and
all three read only what you write here. A thing you leave out cannot be recovered.
A name you generalise away cannot be matched. You are not compressing this
conversation, you are transcribing what it was about.

WHAT MAKES A SUMMARY USABLE

Keep the real nouns. The tree is matched on them.
  weak    "did some algebra"
  better  "factorised a quadratic"
  right   "factorised 4a^2 + 12ab + 9b^2 - 25c^2 as a perfect square minus a square,
           applied x^2 - y^2 = (x+y)(x-y), divided by 2a+3b+5c"
The third names the identity, the method and the expression. The first names nothing
a syllabus tree can match.

Keep every distinct thing. One "covered" entry per thing worked on. A single
photographed question gives one entry. A revision session the night before an exam
can give twenty-five, across two chapters. Do not pick the main one and drop the
rest. Do not merge two different chapters into one line because they were adjacent.

Do not split one thing into its steps. "Found the normal force, then the limiting
friction, then the acceleration" is ONE entry - it is one problem solved in stages.
Two different problems, or two different concepts explained in turn, are two entries.

Separate the ask from the teaching. The student usually types nothing: the photo is
the question, and the only record of it is the bot restating the problem. When the
bot writes "Introduced question 15, isosceles trapezium ABCD, kite ABEF, angles
FAB=43 AFE=137, find x and y", that IS the student's question - transcribe it as the
ask, not as teaching. Where the bot answered something other than what was asked,
say so in "notes"; that is a finding, not noise.

Ignore completely. Tapped reply buttons ("Let's go", "Okay", "Sure thing", "Yes!"),
requests about format or language ("explain step by step", "speak in Tamil", "use a
simpler voice"), the bot's encouragement, and the student's thanks. None of it is
subject matter, and treating any of it as a topic is a failure.

Language. The conversation may be in English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu or a mix, and the
bot sometimes switches mid-answer. Summarise in English whatever the language, but
keep proper nouns, technical terms and literary titles in their original form.

BOT TAUGHT is the bot's own note on each exchange and is usually the cleanest signal.
BOT REPLIES is what it actually said, which is longer and padded with encouragement.
Where they disagree, BOT TAUGHT wins. Where BOT TAUGHT is thin or absent, read BOT
REPLIES carefully - it may be the only record.

BEFORE YOU RETURN, CHECK

- Does every "covered" entry name something concrete enough that someone holding a
  syllabus could find it? If an entry would match fifty different chapters, it is
  too vague - put the real names in "detail".
- Did you cover the end of the conversation as well as the start? Long conversations
  drift, and the last ten exchanges are as real as the first ten.
- Is anything in "covered" actually a reply button, a format request, or politeness?
  Remove it.
- Are two entries the same thing said twice? Merge them. Is one entry secretly two
  different chapters? Split it.

If the conversation is not schoolwork at all, set "not_academic" to one of
"chit chat", "app question", "photo with no schoolwork", "illegible", leave "covered"
empty, and still write an "overview" saying what it was instead.
