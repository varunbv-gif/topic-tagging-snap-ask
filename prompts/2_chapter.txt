Same conversation, same summary. The subject is settled. Now choose the chapter, or
chapters, inside it.

This is the second of three steps. The step after yours sees only the nodes inside
the chapters you return, so a chapter you miss is a topic that can never be found.

Rules
1. Copy chapter names EXACTLY as listed. Do not invent, reword or fix spelling, even
   where a name looks wrong - the tree's spelling is what the output is matched on.
2. Return one chapter per distinct area the summary describes, not one per line of
   it. Several entries about photosynthesis, stomata and the light reaction are all
   Life Processes: one chapter. Entries about photosynthesis and about Broca's area
   are two chapters.
3. ORDER MATTERS. Put first the chapter that accounts for most of the conversation -
   it becomes the conversation's primary tag. Judge by how much was covered, not by
   what came first; a revision session often opens on one chapter and spends most of
   its time in another.
4. About 5% of conversations genuinely cross a chapter. Returning two when the
   conversation covered two is correct. Returning two when it covered one is not.
5. The class, board and tree order below RANK the candidates. They never rule any
   out. A chapter well above or below the student's year is a legal and often
   correct answer - filtering to the student's own year would lose 1 conversation
   in 5 in this dataset.
6. If no chapter here fits, set "reject" to OUT_OF_SCOPE and say in "why" what the
   conversation was actually about, in enough detail to act on. Those rejections are
   the list of chapters the syllabus is missing, so they are worth writing well.
